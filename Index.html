New native vocabulary. The core lexicon (below) is recoined from wider Romance roots — Occitan, Catalan, old Portuguese — so the words are genuinely Latin-descended but almost never the Spanish or Italian word: water → àiga, house → maisó, moon → luèna, word → mot, king → reis, dog → can, old → vèlho. Anything outside the dictionary still runs through the cipher, so any text translates.

Solvàno's own grammar. The sentence shape belongs to no single language. Verbs conjugate and the subject pronoun drops (like Latin/Spanish, but with Solvàno's own endings): I speak → fàulu, she speaks → fàulat, they speak → fàulant — no pronoun needed. The verb goes last (SOV), "the" fuses onto the noun as -te/-ta, adjectives follow the noun, and negation puts non before the (final) verb. So "The old woman gives bread to the king" → Fèmnata vèlha pan a reiste donat, and "She does not know the true name" → Nomte vèro non sabit.

The female register. Women speak the same Solvàno — fully understood by men — but mark it several ways at once: n→ñ (soft palatal), a hushed s→x between vowels, lighter -e→-i endings (nomte→nomti), and their own words for basics — I = jia (vs jeu), me = mia, you = tia, and = ia, yes = ài.

Verb conjugation & pro-drop

Verbs are infinitives in -ar / -er / -ir and conjugate by person; the subject pronoun is dropped (pro-drop). Solvàno keeps the Latin final -t / -nt that Spanish and Italian lost. Auxiliaries (be, have, do, go) and modals stay as fixed words.

Core lexicon & genders

The foundation stones. Nouns are marked m / f; adjectives and determiners agree automatically. Articles: te (m) / ta (f) / tos · tas (plural); un / una for "a"; del / dela for "of the". This is the set to grow.
